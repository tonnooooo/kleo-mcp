There is a giant black hole at the center of our galaxy. So why hasn't it swallowed us?

It packs roughly four million times the mass of our Sun. But mass alone does not tell the whole story.

Black holes do not magically suck everything in. From far away, their gravity depends on mass and distance.

Imagine replacing the Sun with a black hole of exactly the same mass. Earth would keep its orbit.

We would lose sunlight, which would be disastrous for life. But we would not suddenly spiral into the center.

Our galaxy's central black hole is about twenty six thousand light years away. It is not about to swallow Earth.

To understand an orbit, ask about mass, distance, and motion. The words black hole are not enough.

The real mystery begins much closer. What would happen if you crossed the point of no return?
